Night mode: which night?

The states are noted as they are. Here's the same briefing sheet on desktop and the same case on a phone, in three night themes. In all three, colours keep their jobs: magenta is what the AI wrote or decided, blue (cyan in option 2) is what you can click, and the light text is NTSB's. Glyphs and hatching work the same, so nothing relies on colour alone.

1 · Night chart

The same sectional with the lights down: deep navy paper, warm chart-white text, the graticule still faintly there. Closest to the day look; switching feels like dimming the room, not opening a different app.

Choose 1

2 · Glass cockpit

Black, like a modern cockpit display, which is what the B612 typeface was drawn for. It borrows the glass-cockpit colour code: white for data, cyan for what you set, magenta for the course the computer worked out (our AI rule already matches it). Faint range rings replace the grid. The most striking.

Choose 2

3 · Panel

Matte charcoal like an instrument panel. Labels become engraved placards and cards sit in shallow bezels; no grid at all. The quietest for long reading, with the aviation feel carried by the placards rather than the background.
